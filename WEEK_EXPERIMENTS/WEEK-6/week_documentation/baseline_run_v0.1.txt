4. **Repeat**: Steps 2 and 3 are repeated until no more pairs can be identified. This is typically done until a certain threshold is reached, such as a minimum frequency count.
5. **Result**: The final output is a compressed representation of the text, where each character is represented by a unique index or token.

Autoregressive (AR) generation is a type of generative model that uses past observations to predict future values. It is a fundamental concept in time series forecasting and has been widely used in various fields, including finance, economics, and signal processing.

In an autoregressive model, the current value of a time series is predicted based on a weighted sum of past values. The weights are learned during training, and the model uses this information to make predictions about future values.

The general form of an autoregressive model is:

y(t) = ∑[w_k * y(t-k)] + ε(t)

where:

* y(t) is the current value of the time series
* w_k is the weight assigned to the k-th past value
* y(t-k) is the k-th past value
* ε(t) is the error term

The weights w_k are learned during training, and the model uses this information to make predictions about future values.

Autoregressive generation has several advantages, including:

1. **Efficient use of data**: Autoregressive models can be trained on large datasets, making them efficient for time series forecasting.
2. **Robustness to noise**: Autoregressive models can be robust to noise and outliers in the data.
3. **Flexibility**: Autoregressive models can be extended to include additional features, such as seasonality or trends.

However, autoregressive models also have some limitations, including:

1. **Assumes stationarity**: Autoregressive models assume that the time series is stationary, meaning that the distribution of the data does not change over time.
